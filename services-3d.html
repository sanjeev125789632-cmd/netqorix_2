01 / Who this is for

Visualizing industrial, architectural and complex products.

If your product is large, technical, still in design, or simply difficult to photograph well, a 3D model lets you show it clearly. One accurate model can produce catalogue images, a 360° turntable, an animation or a real-time viewer for a website.

Netqorix builds 3D models, textures, rigs, animations and product renders. Sanjeev scopes the work directly with you, so the model is built for how it will actually be used, a web viewer, a still render or an animation, rather than being over-built for the wrong purpose.

A 3D model is a visualization asset, not an engineering drawing. It shows how something looks and moves. It is not a substitute for CAD files used to manufacture the product.
02 / Problems this can address

Expensive studio shoots, internal mechanisms and pre-production demos.

Expensive studio shoots.

Photographing every variant, colour or angle of a product costs time and money. A model is built once and rendered many times.

Internal mechanisms cannot be photographed.

Cutaways, exploded views and moving parts are difficult or impossible to show with a camera.

The product does not exist yet.

You need to show a customer, investor or tender committee something before the prototype is built.

Products too large or awkward to move.

Machinery, plant equipment and installations are hard to bring to a shoot.

Catalogue images do not match.

Photos taken at different times and places look inconsistent. Renders from one model stay consistent.

A website needs a 3D viewer.

A real-time viewer needs a model that is correctly prepared for the web, not one made for a printed brochure.

03 / How it works

Topology, PBR texturing, rigging and GLB/glTF web exports.

Modeling and topology.

The model is built as a mesh of polygons. Topology is how cleanly those polygons are arranged. Clean topology deforms and renders well and keeps file sizes sensible.

PBR texturing.

Physically based rendering (PBR) materials describe colour, roughness, metal and surface detail so the model looks correct under any lighting. Textures are matched to your reference.

Rigging and animation.

A rig is a hidden skeleton of controls that lets a model move. Rigging is added when you need character movement, opening doors, rotating parts or mechanism animation.

Rendering.

A render is a still image or a 360° turntable produced from the finished model with lighting and camera chosen for the product.

Real-time versus high-poly.

A model for a website or game is optimised to stay light. A model for a render can carry far more detail. The end use decides which one you are paying for.

GLB/glTF web exports.

GLB/glTF is the standard format for showing 3D in a browser. Web-bound models are exported in this format and checked for size and loading behaviour.

Tell us the end use first. A model made for a still render usually needs rework before it performs well in a browser, and that rework is a separate cost.

05 / What you get and what is excluded

Asset formats, source files and rendering rights.

Included in the agreed scope:

  • The finished model or render set, built to the approved reference and scope.
  • Source files in .blend, .fbx and .glb formats on delivery.
  • PBR textures and materials with the model, where texturing is in your scope.
  • Web-bound models exported as GLB/glTF and checked before handover.
  • Rendering and usage rights: under the standard engagement terms, graphic assets and IP rights transfer to you on final invoice settlement.
  • Two rounds of revisions per milestone, as set out in the engagement terms.

Not included unless quoted separately:

  • Interactive 3D website development. See website development and deployment (/services-web).
  • Stock models, licensed textures and paid assets, unless they are written into the quote.
  • Engineering, manufacturing or 3D-print-ready files. The model is for visualization.
  • Reverse engineering from poor reference. Accuracy depends on the quality of the drawings, photos and dimensions you supply.
  • Hosting and delivery of web 3D files. See running costs below.
  • Extra revision rounds beyond those included, and changes to approved milestones.
  • Models or features discussed but not written into the approved scope.

If you supply reference images, drawings or branded designs, you confirm you hold the rights to use them. Netqorix does not model from material you are not entitled to share.

07 / The delivery process

Reference intake, clay render milestone and texture signoff.

STEP 01
Reference intake

Share drawings, CAD exports, photos, dimensions and the end use: web viewer, still render or animation. The quality of the reference sets the ceiling on accuracy.

STEP 02
Scope and fixed quote

Sanjeev confirms the option, polygon detail, number of models and deliverables, and issues a fixed quote with exclusions and payment milestones written down.

STEP 03
Clay render milestone

You review an untextured grey render to approve shape and proportions before any texturing begins. Changes are cheapest at this stage.

STEP 04
Texture and material signoff

PBR textures and materials are applied and shown for your approval. Rigging and animation, if in scope, are added after signoff.

STEP 05
Final delivery and handover

Final renders or web exports are delivered with the source files. Rights transfer on final settlement.

Delivery time depends on model complexity, how complete the reference is and how quickly approvals come back. A schedule is confirmed in your quote rather than promised in advance on this page.

Rendering compute and web 3D asset hosting.

One-time project cost:

The work is billed per model or per set at the rate above, 50% to start and 50% on delivery. If the scope changes mid-project, the change is quoted and approved separately before it is made.

Rendering compute:

High-detail photorealistic renders and animations need a lot of processing time. Where a project needs heavy or batch rendering, the compute requirement is stated in your quote before work begins, not added afterwards.

Web 3D asset hosting:

GLB/glTF files shown on a website must be hosted somewhere and delivered quickly. Hosting is billed by your hosting or cloud provider, not by Netqorix. See cloud services and infrastructure (/services-cloud) for managed options.

FAQ

3D modeling questions.

What files will I receive?
Source files in .blend, .fbx and .glb formats are included on delivery, along with textures and final renders. If you need another format, ask before the quote so it is written into the scope.
What is the difference between a model for web and a model for rendering?
A real-time model for a website or game is optimised to stay light and load quickly. A model for a high-quality render can be far more detailed. The two are built differently, so the end use is confirmed before pricing.
Can you model from CAD files, drawings or photos?
Yes. The result is only as accurate as the reference you supply. Dimensions, multiple angles and clear drawings give the best match. Poor or missing reference may need extra time or additional input from you.
Will the render look exactly like my real product?
Renders are matched to your reference for shape, colour and material. Exact identical appearance depends on the quality of that reference, and the clay render and texture signoff stages exist so you can approve the match before final delivery.
What is the difference between 3D modeling and interactive 3D website development?
3D modeling produces the model, textures and renders. Interactive 3D website development builds the web experience that displays a model in a browser. They are priced separately. See website development and deployment for the interactive side.
Who owns the models and renders?
Under the standard engagement terms, graphic assets and IP rights transfer to you on final invoice settlement. Any stock or licensed assets are identified in the quote.
Can you rig and animate a model?
Yes. Complex and character models are built rigging-ready, and Animation / Rigging can be added to any tier. It covers a rig plus animation cycles for the model.
Can the model be used for engineering or manufacturing?
No. The models are for visualization. If you need production-ready engineering files, those come from CAD, not from a visual model.
How long will a model take?
It depends on the detail, the number of materials and how complete your reference is. A schedule is confirmed in your fixed quote, not promised in advance.
