01 / Who this is for

Scalable architecture for growing businesses.

If your website, application or internal tool has outgrown basic shared hosting, or a slow or offline day now costs you money, it needs infrastructure designed for the load. That means a proper cloud setup, with someone responsible for keeping it healthy.

Netqorix sets up and manages cloud infrastructure on AWS, Google Cloud, Azure and DigitalOcean, and deploys on platforms such as Vercel and Netlify. Sanjeev scopes the work directly with you, so you pay for the capacity and care you need now, with room to grow.

A small brochure website rarely needs managed cloud infrastructure. Ordinary hosting is cheaper and enough. If that describes you, you will be told so rather than sold a managed plan.

Downtime, unmanaged costs and fragile deployments.

Most of these come from infrastructure that was set up quickly and never revisited. A designed setup and a managed plan address the cause, not just the latest failure.

03 / How it works

Architecture, VPCs, IAM and automated CI/CD.

Cloud, in everyday terms.

The cloud means renting computing capacity, storage and databases from a provider instead of owning servers. You pay for what you use, and capacity can grow or shrink.

Architecture.

The plan for which services run where and how they connect. A good architecture is sized for your real traffic and costs, not for the largest possible load.

VPCs.

A virtual private cloud is a private, walled-off section of the provider's network where your servers and databases sit, so they are not exposed to the open internet unnecessarily.

IAM.

Identity and access management is the set of rules for who and what is allowed to do what. Giving each person and service only the access they need limits the damage if something goes wrong.

Automated CI/CD.

A pipeline that takes an approved change and puts it live the same way every time, so updates are repeatable and can be rolled back.

Monitoring, backups and scaling.

Health monitoring and alerts catch problems early, backups let you recover, and autoscaling rules add capacity when traffic rises.

Bigger is not better. Over-sized infrastructure wastes money and under-sized infrastructure fails under load. The architecture is chosen to fit your actual use.

05 / What you get and what is excluded

Managed cloud deliverables and provider boundaries.

Included in the agreed scope:

  • Cloud architecture, VPCs, IAM rules and CI/CD pipeline configured as written in your scope.
  • Written documentation of what was set up, so the setup is not held in one person's head.
  • The monitoring, backups, SSL and patching listed for your managed tier.
  • Autoscaling rules, database tuning, alerts and a monthly infrastructure report on the Business tier.
  • Load balancing, DevOps engineering and 24x7 incident monitoring with priority SLA on the Scale tier.
  • Cloud accounts held in your name, with credentials and access handed over.

Not included unless quoted separately:

  • The cloud provider's usage charges, which are billed at cost to you or passed through with the management margin.
  • Application development and bug fixes in your code. See mobile and web app development (/services-app).
  • Any uptime percentage guarantee. Service levels are written into your quote and no figure is promised on this page.
  • Compliance certifications or audits, such as formal security or regulatory certifications.
  • Third-party software licences, domain registration and paid monitoring tools.
  • Migration or data transfer work beyond what is stated in the approved scope.
  • Provider outages. Netqorix manages your setup; the provider runs the underlying platform, and its outages are outside Netqorix's control.

07 / The delivery process

From infrastructure design to production release.

STEP 01
Share what you run

Describe the application, its current hosting, approximate traffic and any problems you have had. Access to the current setup helps.

STEP 02
Design and fixed quote

Sanjeev proposes the provider and architecture, estimates the provider's likely usage charges, and issues a fixed quote for the Netqorix work with deliverables, exclusions and payment milestones.

STEP 03
Build and staging

Networking, access rules and the CI/CD pipeline are built and tested in a staging environment before anything touches production.

STEP 04
Test and go-live audit

The setup is checked for security, backups, monitoring and deployment before it carries live traffic.

STEP 05
Production release and handover

The setup goes live. Documentation, credentials and account access are handed over, and the managed plan begins if you have chosen one.

Timelines depend on the size of the setup, whether existing systems must be moved and how quickly access and approvals come back. A schedule is confirmed in your quote rather than promised in advance on this page.
08 / Pricing, billing and running costs

Provider usage costs vs management retainer.

One-time setup

Cloud Setup is billed per project at the rate above, 50% to start and 50% on delivery. If the scope changes mid-project, the change is quoted and approved separately before it is built.

Management retainer

Managed plans are monthly retainers billed in advance. They pay for Netqorix's time: monitoring, patching, backups, tuning and reporting, according to your tier. They do not include the provider's charges.

Provider usage costs

The provider charges for compute, storage, databases and bandwidth based on what your application uses. There are two ways to handle this. You can pay the provider directly under your own account, with Netqorix charging a fixed monthly management fee. Or Netqorix can pass the provider's cost through with a management margin of 15 to 20%. Which one applies is agreed before work begins.

Keeping costs predictable

Your quote includes an estimate of the provider's likely monthly usage so there is no surprise at the end of the first month. Estimates depend on real usage, and actual charges can differ.

Netqorix itemises these in your quote so you can see the Netqorix fee and the provider's charges as separate lines.

FAQ

Cloud infrastructure questions.

Do I need cloud infrastructure or is normal hosting enough?
A brochure website with modest traffic is usually fine on ordinary hosting. Cloud infrastructure is worth the cost when downtime is expensive, traffic varies, you run an application or database, or you need repeatable deployments. You will get a direct answer, including when the answer is that you do not need it.
Which cloud provider will you use?
Netqorix works with AWS, Google Cloud, Azure and DigitalOcean, and deploys on Vercel and Netlify. The choice depends on your application, your team's familiarity and cost. It is recommended and written into the scope before work begins.
What is the difference between setup and a managed plan?
Setup is a one-time project that designs and builds the infrastructure. A managed plan is the continuing monthly work of watching, patching, backing up and tuning it. Setup alone leaves nobody responsible once it is live.
Who pays the cloud provider's bill?
Either you pay the provider directly under your own account while Netqorix charges a fixed management fee, or Netqorix passes the provider's cost through at cost plus a 15 to 20% management margin. The arrangement is agreed up front.
Who owns the cloud account?
You do. Accounts are held in your name and Netqorix is given the access needed to work on them, so you are never locked in.
What happens if the cloud provider has an outage?
Provider outages are outside Netqorix's control. A well-designed setup reduces how much an outage affects you, and the monitoring on a managed plan means problems are noticed and acted on quickly, but no provider or manager can prevent every outage.
Do you guarantee uptime?
No uptime percentage is promised on this page. Any service levels, such as the priority SLA on the Scale tier, are written into your quote.
Can you move my existing site or application to the cloud?
Yes, as part of a scoped setup. How much work it takes depends on how the current system is built, so migration is described in the scope and priced before it starts.
How is Cloud Services different from Hosting Management under Maintenance & Support?
Hosting Management covers the routine administration of an existing hosted setup. Cloud Services designs and builds the architecture and adds managed tiers with monitoring, scaling and DevOps support. Your quote states which applies to you.
How do you keep my cloud costs under control?
By choosing a size that matches your real use, giving you a monthly usage estimate and reporting on infrastructure from the Business tier upward. Actual charges still depend on how much your application is used.
